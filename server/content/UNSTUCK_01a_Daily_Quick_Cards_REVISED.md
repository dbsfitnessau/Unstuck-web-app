## How to use these cards

These are the day-of references — print them, screenshot them, take them into your session. The full descriptions, cues, contraindications, and the "why" behind each movement sit in **UNSTUCK — Main Program** (Parts 4 and 5). Read the Main Program once before Day 1 — these Cards assume you already know the movements.

**Tier key:**

- **🟢 Recreational** — bodyweight, supported, conservative ranges. Start here if you're new or coming back from time off.
- **🟡 Intermediate** — unsupported, longer holds, deeper ranges.
- **🔴 Athlete** — loaded, end-range, integrated patterns. Earn this tier — don't jump because it sounds harder.

Pick the level that matches **today**, not the level that matches your ego. Staying on 🟢 the whole 28 days is a complete program.

**🛑 Stop signs (read once, remember):**

- Sharp pain → stop.
- Pins-and-needles or numbness → stop and see a physio.
- Pain getting worse rep-to-rep → back off depth or load.
- Joint clicking *with pain* → stop. (Painless clicking is usually fine.)
- *Pain* (not ordinary muscle soreness) lingering >48h → reduce volume next session. DOMS at 48h is normal; pain at 48h isn't.

**Not for** pregnancy or early postpartum, recent surgery, or anyone with a history of disc symptoms attempting **any loaded spinal flexion** (Cat-Cow, Forward Fold, Inchworm, Jefferson Curl, and Child's Pose with Side Reach are all flexion-biased — drop to 🟢, small range, no load regardless of how you feel today). See full disclaimer in the Main Program.

**Method note.** CARs and PAILs/RAILs concepts come from Dr Andreo Spina's *Functional Range Conditioning*. Full attribution in the Main Program.

## The Weekly Schedule

Five training days. Two recovery days. Twenty minutes a day in Weeks 1–2. Twenty-five to thirty in Weeks 3–4 (loaded work simply takes longer — this is honest, not a deal-breaker).

| Day | Focus | Duration (Wk 1–2) | Duration (Wk 3–4) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Day 1** *(Monday)* | Hips & Hamstrings | 20 min | 25–30 min |
| **Day 2** *(Tuesday)* | Thoracic Spine & Shoulders | 20 min | 25–30 min |
| **Day 3** *(Wednesday)* | Full-Body Flow & Breath | 20 min | 25–30 min |
| **Day 4** *(Thursday)* | Ankles & Deep Squat | 20 min | 25–30 min |
| **Day 5** *(Friday)* | Posterior Chain & Wind-Down | 20 min | 25–30 min |
| **Recovery** *(Saturday)* | Active recovery — 30–45 min walk, cycle, or swim | — | — |
| **Rest** *(Sunday)* | Full rest or easy walk | — | — |

You can shift the days to fit your week. What matters is that you do five sessions, with at least one full rest day, and don't skip the active recovery.

**When to slot mobility around your other training:**

- If you train in the **morning**, do mobility in the **evening** (or before bed) — keeps you fresh for the lift.
- If you train in the **afternoon or evening**, do mobility in the **morning** — primes the joints for later loading.
- Avoid stacking a long deep-squat hold immediately before heavy squats. End-range mobility temporarily reduces stiffness, which you actually want for max strength.

## WEEKS 1–2 — FOUNDATION (20 minutes)

**The goal of Weeks 1–2:** introduce the positions, build breath capacity in end ranges, re-establish positions your nervous system has forgotten about, and learn CARs as a daily input.

**Effort feel:** 6/10. You should feel worked, not wrecked. If you finish a session feeling beaten up, you've gone too hard — back off the depth and the holds, not the consistency.

**Warm-up:** No separate warm-up in Weeks 1–2. The Hip / Shoulder / Ankle CARs that open each session double as the warm-up — that's deliberate.

Do **Week 1** as written. Repeat the same sessions for **Week 2** — but add 15 seconds to every static hold under 60s (or 30 seconds to holds of 60–90s) and one rep to every active set. Tighten up on form, not on speed.

If Week 2 still feels hard, that's fine — stay on 🟢 and run the foundation routines for a third week before progressing. Better to under-progress and move clean than rush into Week 3 with incorrect positions and poor form.

## WEEKS 3–4 — PROGRESSION (25–30 minutes)

**The goal of Weeks 3–4:** load the positions you've opened up, train end-range control, and integrate mobility into athletic patterns.

**Effort feel:** 7–8/10. You should feel challenged. End-range work is uncomfortable — that's the dose.

**Session length: 25–30 minutes.** Loaded work and PAILs/RAILs cycles take longer than holds. Plan for it. If you only have 25 minutes, prioritise the CARs opener, the loaded lift, and one PAILs/RAILs cycle — skip non-essential, low-intensity movements at the end of the session.

**Before each Week 3–4 session:** spend **2–3 minutes warming up generally** — brisk walk, 30 jumping jacks, or 1–2 minutes of skipping. CARs are not a substitute for raising tissue temperature before loaded end-range work.

### Progression principles

You'll keep the same five-day structure (Hips & Hamstrings, Thoracic Spine & Shoulders, Full-Body Flow, Ankles & Deep Squat, Posterior Chain). What changes is the **intensity** and **complexity**.

Two new tools layer onto CARs:

**1. PAILs/RAILs — Progressive and Regressive Angular Isometric Loading.** At the end-range of a stretch, push the *stretched tissue* into the floor or wall (PAILs) for 20 seconds at moderate effort (50–60%), then actively pull yourself deeper into the stretch using the *opposite* muscles (RAILs) for 20 seconds. By the second cycle it should feel "very hard but controlled." This trains the nervous system to produce force at end range — closing the gap between passive and active range. *(Method: Spina / FRC.)*

**2. Loaded Mobility.** Adding a kettlebell, dumbbell, or band turns a stretch into a strength position. Loaded end-range work is what creates *durable* range — the kind that sticks.

## A note on switching between cards

If today's session feels harder than yesterday's — sleep, stress, illness, training load — drop a tier. If it feels easier, hold the tier; only step up when the current tier feels controlled and clean for two sessions in a row.

The card is a guide. Your body is the source.
