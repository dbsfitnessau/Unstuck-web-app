## A Note Before You Start

This isn't a stretching programme.

Stretching is what you do for thirty seconds before training, panicking about a tight hamstring, and then forget about it for the rest of the week. Mobility is different. Mobility is the **usable range** you own — the positions you can actively get into, control, and produce force from.

This is a 28-day plan to expand that range. Twenty minutes a day in Weeks 1–2, twenty-five to thirty in Weeks 3–4, five days a week, with two days for recovery and walking. By the end you will move into deeper positions, hold them under load, and stop feeling like a rusty hinge before squats.

If you do the work, this works.

Don't Be Sh\*t. Open the page.

## Disclaimer

This programme is general education and is not a substitute for individual medical advice. If you have a current injury, recent surgery, or any condition affecting your joints, nerves, or connective tissue, see a qualified physiotherapist before starting.

**This programme is not designed for pregnancy or the early postpartum period in its current form.** Several positions — frog, deep squat under load, all prone work (cobra, child's pose), supine breath holds, and the Jefferson curl — are contraindicated or require substitution. If you are pregnant or within 12 weeks postpartum, please work one-to-one with a qualified women's health physiotherapist before attempting any version of this plan, and consider waiting for the dedicated pregnancy/postpartum companion (in development).

**If you have a history of disc-related back symptoms** — herniation, sciatica, or recurring acute episodes — treat all spinal flexion movements (Cat-Cow, Standing Forward Fold, Inchworm, Jefferson Curl, Child's Pose with Side Reach) as 🟢 Recreational. Small range. No load. Regardless of how you feel today. The Jefferson Curl section in Part 5 has a hard skip rule — read it.

Pain that is sharp, radiating, or progressive is a stop sign — not a challenge.

**Method attribution.** The CARs (Controlled Articular Rotations) and PAILs/RAILs (Progressive/Regressive Angular Isometric Loading) concepts referenced in this programme were developed by Dr. Andreo Spina under the *Functional Range Conditioning* system (functionalrangeconditioning.com). The implementations here follow the principles of that work.

## How to Use This Programme

This guide has eight parts:

1. **The Why** — the science behind why mobility responds to training and how this programme is structured.
2. **Self-Assessment** — seven tests you'll run before Day 1 and re-run on Day 28.
3. **The Weekly Schedule** — what you do, on which days, for how long.
4. **Weeks 1–2: Foundation** — the daily routines that build a baseline.
5. **Weeks 3–4: Progression** — the loaded, end-range and integrated work.
6. **Day 28: Re-Test** — same tests, side-by-side comparison.
7. **The Maintenance Plan** — what to do once you've finished.
8. **Daily Quick Cards** — one-page-per-day printables to take into your session.

Every exercise is built with three levels. The colour tiers are editorial labels, not clinical categories — they signal where to start, not what's "safe" or "unsafe":

- **🟢 Recreational** — bodyweight, supported, conservative ranges. Start here if you're new to mobility work or coming back from time off.
- **🟡 Intermediate** — unsupported, longer holds, deeper ranges.
- **🔴 Athlete** — loaded, end-range, integrated patterns. Earn this tier — don't jump to it because it sounds harder.

**When to step up a tier:** only when the current tier feels *controlled and clean for two sessions in a row*. Dropping a tier on a tired, stressed, or under-slept day is not regression — it's good coaching. Staying on 🟢 the whole 28 days is a complete program.

**Required:**

- A mat or carpet
- A foam roller (or rolled-up large towel)
- A yoga block or thick book
- A broomstick
- A wall and doorway (you have one)
- A long resistance band (light to moderate, see note below)

**For Weeks 3–4 — recommended load set:**

* **2.5kg plate or dumbbell** — Thoracic Spine extension 🟡, Jefferson Curl 🟡, seated Figure-4 🟡
* **5kg plate or dumbbell** — Thoracic Spine extension 🔴, Jefferson Curl 🔴, Cossack with overhead reach 🔴, seated Figure-4 🔴
* **One mid-range kettlebell or dumbbell, 4–8kg** — KB Halo 🟡, ATG split squat 🟡, loaded Cossack 🟡, single-leg RDL 🟡
* **One moderate kettlebell or dumbbell, 8–12kg** — KB Halo 🔴, single-leg RDL 🔴, ATG split squat 🔴 (held as two 4–8kg DBs in each hand, or one 8–12kg goblet-style), and the lower end of loaded Cossack 🔴 and loaded deep squat 🔴
* **A heavier kettlebell, 12–16kg** *(optional)* — top end of loaded Cossack 🔴 and loaded deep squat 🔴

> **Note on the ATG split squat load:** the kit list above lists 4–8kg as the "mid-range" weight. The 🔴 prescription is **4–8kg in each hand** (i.e. 8–16kg total). If you only own one weight, hold it goblet-style at the chest instead.

**Don't have weights?** Substitutions: a backpack loaded with books (weigh it on a bathroom scale), water-filled detergent jugs (a 5L jug ≈ 5kg), or any sturdy bag you can hold to the chest. The principle — controlled load — matters more than the exact kit.

**Resistance band:** for narrow-grip pass-throughs in Week 3–4 Day 2, you'll need a band of at least 90cm circumference (≈180cm flat). Standard short therapy bands are too short.

**If you only own one weight,** prioritise a **10–12kg kettlebell**. That covers the lower end of every 🔴 prescription (Cossack, Halo, goblet squat, RDL, deep squat hold) without forcing you to over-load the lighter exercises. Run lighter movements (Thoracic extension, Jefferson curl, seated Figure-4) at bodyweight.

## 🛑 Stop Signs (read once, remember for 28 days)

While training:

- **Sharp, stabbing pain** in any position → stop immediately. Drop the level by one tier or skip the exercise.
- **Radiating pain, pins-and-needles, or numbness** → stop and see a physio. Don't push through nerve symptoms.
- **Pain that gets worse rep-to-rep** → that's not "working through it." Back off the depth or the load.
- **Joint clicking with pain** → stop. (Painless clicking is usually fine.)
- **Pain (not ordinary muscle soreness) that lingers \>48 hours after a session**, or soreness that worsens day-on-day → reduce volume next session and reassess. DOMS at 48 hours is normal; *pain* at 48 hours isn't.

Mobility work should be uncomfortable at end range. It should not be painful in the medical sense. Learn the difference.

## Part 1 — The Why

### Mobility is trainable

Range of motion is governed by three things, working in combination:

**1. Your nervous system.** A significant part of what we feel as "tightness" is neural — a stretch-tolerance response, not a permanently short muscle. Train the position with intent and breath, and the nervous system raises its tolerance for that position. This is why you can often push past your "limit" after a few minutes of slow, breathed work. (See Weppler & Magnusson, 2010, *Phys Ther* — "Increasing muscle extensibility: a matter of increasing length or modifying sensation?")

**2. The joint capsule and connective tissue.** Joint capsules, tendons and ligaments adapt to the loads and angles you put through them, but on a slower timescale than neural change. If you only ever sit, walk, and squat to parallel, your hip capsule treats that range as the whole job. End-range work over weeks tells the capsule to remodel (Bohm, Mersmann & Arampatzis, 2015, *Sports Med Open*).

**3. Active control at end range.** Passive range (what someone can push you into) is not the same as active range (what you can produce yourself). End-range strength is protective against some specific non-contact injuries — eccentric hamstring work, for example, roughly halves hamstring strain incidence in soccer (Petersen et al., 2011, *Am J Sports Med*). Closing the passive-active gap is a core aim of this programme, alongside the neural and structural pieces above.

### Why 28 days

Twenty-eight days is enough to produce noticeable **neural adaptation** (stretch tolerance, motor control, position familiarity) and the **beginnings of structural change**. Full tendon and capsule remodelling continues well past Day 28 — typically out to 8–12+ weeks of consistent loading (Bohm et al., 2015). That's why the maintenance plan in Part 7 matters as much as the first 28 days. Twenty-eight days gets the change started; maintenance is what compounds it.

### Why this structure

The programme follows a **build → load → integrate** arc:

- **Weeks 1–2 (Foundation):** isolated positions, breath work, baseline ranges, and the introduction of CARs (Controlled Articular Rotations) on the joint-specific days. The nervous system learns the positions are safe.
- **Weeks 3–4 (Progression):** load and end-range. Capsules and tendons begin to remodel. Active control is forced through PAILs/RAILs and external load.
- **Week 5 → (Maintenance):** a weekly loop that holds the gains. Mobility gains regress without ongoing input — most detraining literature shows meaningful loss of joint range and tendon stiffness within 4–12 weeks of stopping stimulus. Maintenance isn't optional.

You will train five days a week. Two days are for active recovery — walking, easy cycling, swimming. Recovery is when adaptation actually happens. Don't skip it.

## Part 2 — Self-Assessment

Run all seven of these tests on **Day 0** (the day before you start) and again on **Day 29** (the morning after Week 4's last session). Photograph or video each test from the same angle both times. The "after" comparison is the most satisfying part of the programme — don't skip the baseline or you lose the proof.

Record everything in the **Progress Worksheet** (separate document).

> **A note on self-administered tests.** Tests 5 (Thomas) and 6 (thoracic rotation) are normally administered by a clinician. Self-administered, they're directionally useful but less precise. Use them to track *your own change over time*, not to compare yourself against population norms.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side panel with seven small icons, one per test, in a row.\]

### Test 1 — Deep Squat Hold

**How:** Feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out. Drop into the deepest squat you can hold without your heels lifting or your back rounding. Hold the position as long as form stays clean — *up to 60 seconds*. Stop when any of the form criteria below break, or when you reach 60s, whichever comes first.

**Measure:**

* Heels stay down throughout? (Y/N)
* Knees track over toes throughout? (Y/N)
* Spine stays long throughout? (Y/N)
* Hold time before form breaks, or 60s+ if form held the whole time (seconds)

**Why:** Tells us about ankle, hip, and thoracic mobility in one shot.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on figure in deep squat with annotation lines on heels, knees, spine.\]

### Test 2 — Wall Ankle Test

**How:** Kneel facing a wall in a half-kneeling position. Slide your front foot back until your knee can just barely touch the wall without the heel lifting. Measure from the wall to the tip of your big toe in centimetres — use a tape measure or your phone's measure app. Test both sides.

**Measure:** Distance in cm, left and right.

**Why:** Ankle dorsiflexion is a major contributor to squat depth, lunge mechanics, and running economy.

**Benchmark:** Normative knee-to-wall distance in healthy adults sits around 9–13cm (Konor et al., 2012, *Int J Sports Phys Ther*; Bennell et al., 1998). We use **10cm+ as a working "functional" threshold** and **<5cm as clearly restricted**. Side-to-side asymmetry greater than ~1.5–2cm is worth flagging — even if both sides hit the benchmark, big asymmetries predict altered loading.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on half kneel, knee toward wall, ruler/cm marker between toe and wall.\]

### Test 3 — Sit and Reach (Modified)

**How:** Sit on the floor, legs straight in front, feet flexed against a wall or box. Reach forward without rounding your low back hard. Mark how far past your toes (or short of) your fingertips reach.

**Measure:** Distance past or short of toes (cm).

**Benchmark:** Fingertips **level with your toes is the neutral mark** — not restricted, not mobile, just even. The scorecard treats that as **5/10**, with 15cm past your toes scoring 10/10 and 15cm short scoring 0/10. Be careful comparing this number with anyone else's: sit-and-reach is strongly affected by the ratio of your arm length to your leg and torso length, so two people with identical hamstring length can score centimetres apart. Track your own change across 28 days — that delta is the honest signal, not the absolute number.

**Why:** Hamstring length and lumbar/thoracic flexion combined.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on long-sit reach with measuring marker.\]

### Test 4 — Wall Shoulder Flexion

**How:** Stand with your back, head, bum, **and heels** against a wall. Arms straight, raise them overhead trying to touch the wall above your head without your low back arching off the wall.

**Measure:**

- Hands touch the wall? (Y/N)
- Distance from wrist to wall (cm) if not.
- Low back stays in contact? (Y/N)

**Why:** Shoulder flexion + thoracic extension. Important for overhead lifting and posture.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on figure against wall, arms overhead.\]

### Test 5 — Thomas Test (Self-Administered)

**How:** Sit on the edge of a stable bench or bed. Hug one knee tight to your chest and roll back until you're lying flat, that knee held to your chest. The other leg hangs free off the edge. Look at the hanging leg.

**Measure:**

- Hanging thigh sits at or below horizontal? (Y/N) — *measures hip flexor length*
- Knee bends to ~80° **at the knee joint** (the calf hangs back toward the bench leg) without you forcing it? (Y/N) — *measures rectus femoris (front-of-thigh) length, per Kendall & McCreary criteria*
- **If the thigh sits above horizontal, how far above? (°, each leg.)** "Horizontal" here means **the line of the bench top**. Photograph each hanging leg with the phone off to one side, roughly level with your hip, so the thigh and the bench edge are both in shot. If the thigh rests on that line or drops below it, that leg is **0°** — nothing to measure. If it sits above the line, a tight hip flexor is holding it up: measure the angle between the thigh and the bench line on the photo with a free protractor app. Most people land between 0° and 25°, and **smaller is better**.
- **Your score for this test is the average of the two legs** — that's hip flexor length, and it's the number that should come down over 28 days.
- **The difference between the two legs** tells you whether one hip is tighter than the other — e.g. 18° and 6° is a 12° difference. Under ~5° is even enough to ignore. Record each leg and let the app do both sums; don't work them out in your head.

**Why:** Hip flexor and quad length. Sitting all day shortens these — this is the test that tells you by how much. Self-administered, reliability is moderate at best — use it to track yourself over time, not to grade yourself.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on lying figure with one knee to chest, one leg hanging. Horizontal dashed line extending from the bench top, labelled "horizontal". The hanging thigh sits above it, with the thigh-to-line angle marked as the value to record; knee bend angle marked separately. Small inset: thigh resting on the line, labelled "0°".\]

### Test 6 — Seated Thoracic Rotation

**How:** Sit cross-legged with a broomstick across your collarbones, holding it in place with both hands. Rotate as far as you can to one side, then the other, without your hips lifting or shifting.

**Measure:** Estimate rotation angle (use a phone protractor app if you have one — or estimate against a clock face: 12 o'clock = 0° from straight ahead, 1 o'clock ≈ 30°, 2 o'clock ≈ 60°). Healthy adult seated thoracic rotation is roughly 30–45° per side; 45°+ is a strong target for lifters and overhead athletes.

**Why:** Thoracic rotation matters for shoulder health, golf, throwing, and Olympic lifting.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Top-down view of seated rotation with angle marker.\]

### Test 7 — Cossack Squat Depth

**How:** Wide stance, feet pointing forward and slightly out. Shift weight to one side, sinking the loaded hip down while the other leg straightens with the foot flexed. Hold the bottom for 3 seconds, then switch.

**Measure:**

- Loaded heel stays down? (Y/N)
- Loaded hip below the knee? (Y/N)
- Straight leg fully extended with foot flexed? (Y/N)

**Why:** A strong combined test of usable lateral hip range, adductor extensibility, and frontal-plane ankle dorsiflexion.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Front-on figure in Cossack squat, loaded side annotated.\]

## Part 3 — The Weekly Schedule

Five training days. Two recovery days. Twenty minutes a day in Weeks 1–2. Twenty-five to thirty in Weeks 3–4 (loaded work simply takes longer — this is honest, not a deal-breaker).

| Day | Focus | Duration (Wk 1–2) | Duration (Wk 3–4) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Day 1** *(Monday)* | Hips & Hamstrings | 20 min | 25–30 min |
| **Day 2** *(Tuesday)* | Thoracic Spine & Shoulders | 20 min | 25–30 min |
| **Day 3** *(Wednesday)* | Full-Body Flow & Breath | 20 min | 25–30 min |
| **Day 4** *(Thursday)* | Ankles & Deep Squat | 20 min | 25–30 min |
| **Day 5** *(Friday)* | Posterior Chain & Wind-Down | 20 min | 25–30 min |
| **Recovery** *(Saturday)* | Active recovery — 30–45 min walk, cycle, or swim | — | — |
| **Rest** *(Sunday)* | Full rest or easy walk | — | — |

You can shift the days to fit your week. What matters is that you do five sessions, with at least one full rest day, and don't skip the active recovery.

**When to slot mobility around your other training:**

- If you train in the **morning**, do mobility in the **evening** (or before bed) — keeps you fresh for the lift.
- If you train in the **afternoon or evening**, do mobility in the **morning** — primes the joints for later loading.
- Avoid stacking a long deep-squat hold immediately before heavy squats. End-range mobility temporarily reduces stiffness, which you actually want for max strength.

## Part 4 — Weeks 1–2: Foundation

**The goal of Weeks 1–2:** introduce the positions, build breath capacity in end ranges, re-establish positions your nervous system has forgotten about, and learn CARs as a daily input.

**Effort feel:** 6/10. You should feel worked, not wrecked. If you finish a session feeling beaten up, you've gone too hard — back off the depth and the holds, not the consistency.

**Week 1** — do as written.

**Week 2** — repeat the same sessions as Week 1, but add 15 seconds to every static hold under 60s (or 30 seconds to holds of 60–90s) and one rep to every active set. Tighten up on form, not on speed.

If Week 2 still feels hard, that's fine — stay on 🟢 and run the foundation routines for a third week before progressing. Better to under-progress and move clean than rush into Week 3 with incorrect positions and poor form.

**Warm-up:** No separate warm-up is needed in Weeks 1–2. The Hip / Shoulder / Ankle CARs that open each session double as the warm-up — that's deliberate. From Week 3 onward, when load enters the picture, you'll add 2–3 minutes of general warm-up before each session.

### DAY 1 (Week 1-2) — Hips & Hamstrings

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Header image: outline figure mid-90/90 switch.\]

**Why this session:** Hips drive nearly every athletic movement and most desk-related stiffness. We open from spine to glutes to hamstrings in a single flow.

**Total time:** 20 minutes.

---

**Exercise 1 — Hip CARs (Controlled Articular Rotations)**

*Sets/Reps:* 5 slow circles in each direction, each leg. ~90 seconds total.

*How:* On hands and knees. Lift one knee out to the side at 90°, then circle it forward, up, and back behind you, returning to the start. Move *slowly* and with deliberate tension — make the joint do the work, not momentum.

*Key focus:* The other three limbs stay still. Pelvis stays level. The slower you go, the more it does.

*Why:* CARs train the joint to own its full active range. A foundational input from Functional Range Conditioning (Spina). You'll meet them again in Weeks 3–4 with more layers, but the daily input starts now.

*🟢 Recreational:* Smaller circles, hand on a chair for support if needed.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written, full active range.
*🔴 Athlete:* Pause for 2 seconds at the top of each circle (max external rotation), breathing into the position.

*Contraindications:* Recent hip surgery or labral repair — clear with your physio first.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on figure on all fours, leg arcing through CAR pattern.\]

---

**Exercise 2 — Cat-Cow**

*Sets/Reps:* 8–10 controlled reps (≈60 seconds at one full breath per rep)

*How:* On hands and knees. Inhale, drop the belly, lift the chest and tailbone (cow). Exhale, round through the spine, tuck the tailbone, push the floor away (cat). Move slowly — one full breath per rep.

*Key focus:* Move *segment by segment*, not as a block. Imagine each vertebra rolling like a chain.

*Why:* Wakes up segmental spinal control. The spine drives everything below it.

*🟢 Recreational:* As written.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Add a 2-second pause at end of each phase.
*🔴 Athlete:* Combine with thoracic rotations — every 3 reps, drop one elbow under the other arm and rotate.

*Contraindications:* Recent disc-related low back episode. If a position spikes pain, reduce range. (See history-of-disc rule on the disclaimer page.)

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Two side-on figures: one in cat, one in cow.\]

---

**Exercise 3 — World's Greatest Stretch**

*Sets/Reps:* 5 reps each side.

*How:* From a push-up position, step your right foot outside your right hand. Lower the right elbow toward the inside of the right foot — let it touch the floor if you have the range, hover if you don't. Hold one breath. Then rotate the right arm up to the ceiling, eyes following the hand. Return and switch.

*Key focus:* Keep the back leg long and the back knee off the floor. Rotation comes from the mid-back, not the lower back.

*Why:* Hits hip flexor, adductor, hamstring, and Thoracic Spine in one movement. One of the highest-value full-body warm-up patterns.

*🟢 Recreational:* Drop the back knee to the floor.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Add a 5-second hold at end-range rotation.

*Contraindications:* Recent hip flexor strain.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Three-frame sequence: lunge entry, elbow drop, rotation reach.\]

---

**Exercise 4 — 90/90 Hip Switch**

*Sets/Reps:* 8 reps each direction.

*How:* Sit on the floor. Front leg bent 90° in front of you, back leg bent 90° to the side. Both shins on the floor. Sit tall. Lift both knees, rotate to the other side, place both shins down. That's one rep.

*Key focus:* Sit *upright* — don't collapse forward. The work is the rotation, not the depth.

*Why:* Trains internal and external hip rotation — a commonly under-developed range in lifters and runners.

*🟢 Recreational:* Hands on the floor for support, smaller range.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Hands off, sit tall.
*🔴 Athlete:* Lift the front knee toward the chest at the top of each switch (active CAR).

*Contraindications:* Recent knee meniscus injury. Use a cushion under the knees if needed. Use caution if a history of meniscus issues.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Top-down view of 90/90 with arrow showing switch direction.\]

---

**Exercise 5 — Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch**

*Sets/Reps:* 60 seconds each side.

*How:* Half-kneeling position (use a pad under the kneeling knee). Squeeze the back glute hard. Tuck your tailbone under (posterior pelvic tilt). Only then shift slightly forward. Reach the same-side arm overhead and bend slightly to the opposite side.

*Key focus:* The stretch happens in the *front* of the back hip. If you feel it in your low back, you haven't tucked the tailbone enough.

*Why:* Sitting shortens the hip flexors. Short hip flexors tilt the pelvis forward and load the lumbar spine. This addresses that input.

*🟢 Recreational:* Skip the overhead reach. Keep hands on hips.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Add a contract-relax — push the back knee into the floor for 5s, relax for 10s, repeat 3 times within the hold.

*Contraindications:* Recent anterior hip impingement or hip flexor strain. Use caution if a history of either — reduce range and avoid forcing forward.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on half-kneel with overhead reach.\]

---

**Exercise 6 — Frog Stretch**

*Sets/Reps:* 60–90 second hold, 2 rounds.

*How:* On hands and knees. Widen the knees as far as comfortable, in line with the hips, and feet in line with the knees. Lower the hips back toward the heels and drop onto the forearms. Breathe slowly.

*Key focus:* Long, slow exhales. This position only opens when the nervous system relaxes — forcing it does nothing.

*Why:* Opens the adductors and inner hip capsule, which gate squat depth and lateral movement.

*🟢 Recreational:* Knees narrower, hips higher.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Add a slow hip rock — shift the hips forward and back through the available range, 8 slow reps.

*Contraindications:* Recent inner thigh strain. Do not perform during pregnancy without physio clearance (see disclaimer).

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Front-on figure on forearms in frog position.\]

---

**Exercise 7 — Seated Figure-4**

*Sets/Reps:* 60 seconds each side.

*How:* Sit with one foot flat on the floor, knee bent. Cross the other ankle over the standing thigh, just above the knee. Sit tall and lean forward at the hips (not the spine).

*Key focus:* Long spine. Hinge from the hip crease.

*Why:* Targets the deep external rotators (piriformis and friends) which take the brunt of all-day sitting.

*🟢 Recreational:* Lying on your back, pull the knee toward the opposite shoulder.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Add a 5 second active push of the crossed knee away from you (gluteal contraction), 5s relaxation. 4 cycles per side.

*Contraindications:* Recent sciatic nerve symptoms — reduce range, breathe through it, stop if symptoms increase.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Seated figure with one ankle crossed over opposite knee, hinging forward.\]

### DAY 2 (Weeks 1-2) — Thoracic Spine & Shoulders

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Header image: outline figure in thread-the-needle.\]

**Why this session:** The thoracic spine should rotate and extend freely. For many people it doesn't, so the lower back and shoulders pick up the slack — and pay for it.

**Total time:** 20 minutes.

---

**Exercise 1 — Shoulder CARs (Controlled Articular Rotations)**

*Sets/Reps:* 5 slow circles in each direction, each arm. ~90 seconds total.

*How:* Standing tall. Reach one arm forward, then up overhead, then back behind you, then down — drawing the biggest, slowest circle the joint allows. Keep the rest of the body locked in place. Reverse direction.

*Key focus:* Rib cage stays still. Pelvis stays still. Only the shoulder joint moves. If your trunk shifts, you've cheated.

*Why:* CARs train the shoulder to own its full active range — exactly what protects you when you reach overhead under load.

*🟢 Recreational:* Smaller circles, focus on quality over range.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written, full active range.
*🔴 Athlete:* Add a 2-second pause at the top of each circle (max external rotation overhead), breathing into the position.

*Contraindications:* Recent shoulder dislocation or repair — clear with your physio. History of anterior shoulder instability — keep circles within comfortable range.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Standing figure with arrow showing the arm circle path.\]

---

**Exercise 2 — Cat-Cow with Breath**

*Sets/Reps:* 8–10 controlled reps (≈60 seconds at one full breath per rep)

*How:* As Day 1, but matched to the longest, slowest breath you can produce. Inhale 4 seconds into cow, exhale 6 seconds into cat.

*Key focus:* Diaphragm leads, spine follows.

*Why:* Combines spinal warm-up with slow-paced breathing — slow exhales bias the parasympathetic nervous system and prime it for end-range work (Russo, Santarelli & O'Rourke, 2017, *Breathe*).

*🟢 Recreational:* As written.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Add a 2-second pause at end of each phase.
*🔴 Athlete:* Combine with thoracic rotations — every 3 reps, drop one elbow under the other arm and rotate.

*Contraindications:* Recent disc-related low back episode. (See disclaimer.)

**Exercise 3 — Thread the Needle**

*Sets/Reps:* 8 reps each side.

*How:* On hands and knees. Reach the right arm under the body, threading it past the left arm. Lower the right shoulder and ear toward the floor. Return to start by reaching the right arm up to the ceiling, opening the chest.

*Key focus:* The reach up is as important as the reach under. Both directions train rotation.

*Why:* Thoracic rotation in a stable closed-chain position.

*🟢 Recreational:* Keep the supporting elbow soft.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written, with a 3s hold at end of rotation.
*🔴 Athlete:* Open-arm version — supporting hand behind the head, drive the elbow up to the ceiling.

*Contraindications:* Wrist pain — drop to forearms.

**Exercise 4 — Thoracic Extension Over Roller**

*Sets/Reps:* 60 seconds, 3 rounds.

*How:* Foam roller (or rolled towel) horizontally under the upper back at shoulder-blade level. Hands behind the head supporting the neck. Slowly arch back over the roller, breathe out, return. Move the roller up one segment and repeat.

*Key focus:* Extend through the *upper* back, not the low back. The hips stay grounded.

*Why:* Restores thoracic extension lost to desk and phone posture.

*🟢 Recreational:* Smaller range, support the head fully.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Add a light weight (2.5–5kg plate) held to the chest.

*Contraindications:* Osteoporosis, recent rib injury, history of fragility fractures. Use caution if a history of either.

**Exercise 5 — Quadruped Thoracic Spine Rotation**

*Sets/Reps:* 8 reps each side.

*How:* On hands and knees. Place one hand behind the head. Drive that elbow up toward the ceiling, rotating through the mid-back. Then drive it under and across the body.

*Key focus:* Hips stay square. The rotation is *thoracic* — if your hips swing, you've cheated.

*Why:* Isolates Thoracic Spine rotation away from the lumbar spine.

*🟢 Recreational:* Smaller range, slow.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Add a 3 second hold at top of rotation, breathing into the side ribs.

*Contraindications:* Wrist pain — drop to forearms.

**Exercise 6 — Wall Slide / Banded Pass-Through**

*Sets/Reps:* 10 reps, 2 rounds.

*How (wall slide):* Stand with back, head, bum against a wall. Arms in goalpost position, elbows and wrists touching the wall. Slide arms up overhead while keeping all four points on the wall, then slide back down.
*How (band pass-through):* Hold a band wide overhead. Pass the band over the head and behind the back without bending the elbows. Reverse.

*Key focus:* Don't compensate by arching the low back off the wall, or by flaring the ribs.

*Why:* Trains shoulder flexion and external rotation in a position your nervous system trusts.

*🟢 Recreational:* Wall slide, narrower range.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Wall slide, full range.
*🔴 Athlete:* Banded pass-through with a tight grip width.

*Contraindications:* Recent shoulder impingement or anterior instability. Use caution if a history of either.

**Exercise 7 — Doorway Pec Stretch**

*Sets/Reps:* 60 seconds each side.

*How:* Stand in a doorway. Place one forearm against the frame, elbow at shoulder height (90°). Step the same-side foot through the door and rotate the chest away.

*Key focus:* Squeeze the shoulder blade *back and down* while you stretch. Stretching a slack pec doesn't change much.

*Why:* Opens the chest after a day of forward posture and balances the shoulder slide work.

*🟢 Recreational:* Lower elbow position, smaller rotation.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written.

### DAY 3 (Weeks 1-2) — Full-Body Flow

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Header image: figure in down-dog to cobra transition.\]

**Why this session:** Day 3 connects everything. We move continuously, finish with breath work, and treat this as the lower-intensity mid-week session.

**Total time:** 20 minutes.

---

**Exercise 1 — Down Dog → Cobra Flow**

*Sets/Reps:* 8 controlled reps.

*How:* From down dog (hips up, hands and feet planted, body in an inverted V), shift forward over your hands and lower into cobra (hips down, chest up, arms supporting). Reverse.

*Key focus:* One slow breath per phase. Push the floor away in down dog. In cobra, draw the chest *forward* and *up*, not just up.

*Why:* Full-body flow that links shoulders, Thoracic Spine, hips, and hamstrings in a single rhythm.

*🟢 Recreational:* Drop knees to floor in cobra; bend knees in down dog.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Add a 3s hold at peak of each pose, with full breath.

*Contraindications:* Recent wrist or low back pain — substitute cobra to baby cobra (sphinx). History of disc symptoms — keep cobra conservative or substitute sphinx.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on two-frame: down dog and cobra.\]

---

**Exercise 2 — Inchworm to Squat Reach**

*Sets/Reps:* 8 reps.

*How:* Stand tall. Hinge forward and walk the hands out to plank. Walk the feet up to the hands. From there, drop into a deep squat hold and reach one arm to the ceiling. Return and repeat, alternating reach arms.

*Key focus:* Soft knees on the walkout. In the squat, lift the chest and rotate from the Thoracic Spine.

*Why:* Connects posterior chain, plank stability, deep squat, and rotation in one rep.

*🟢 Recreational:* Skip the squat reach, just stand back up.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Add a push-up at the plank position.

*Contraindications:* Recent low back pain on flexion. (See history-of-disc rule on the disclaimer page.)

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Four-frame sequence: stand, walkout, squat, reach.\]

---

**Exercise 3 — World's Greatest Stretch**

*Sets/Reps:* 5 reps each side.

*How:* From a push-up position, step your right foot outside your right hand. Lower the right elbow toward the inside of the right foot — let it touch the floor if you have the range, hover if you don't. Hold one breath. Then rotate the right arm up to the ceiling, eyes following the hand. Return and switch.

*Key focus:* Keep the back leg long and the back knee off the floor. Rotation comes from the mid-back, not the lower back.

*Why:* Hits hip flexor, adductor, hamstring, and Thoracic Spine in one movement. One of the highest-value full-body warm-up patterns.

*🟢 Recreational:* Drop the back knee to the floor.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Add a 5-second hold at end-range rotation.

*Contraindications:* Recent hip flexor strain.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Three-frame sequence: lunge entry, elbow drop, rotation reach.\]

---

**Exercise 4 — Cossack Squat**

*Sets/Reps:* 5 each side, 3-second pause at the bottom. *(This is similar to Test 7 — you're now training the position.)*

*How:* Wide stance, feet pointing forward and slightly out. Shift weight to one side, sinking the loaded hip down. The other leg straightens with the foot flexed. Hold the bottom three seconds, then shift to the other side.

*Key focus:* Loaded heel down. Straight leg fully extended with the foot flexed (heel only on the floor).

*Why:* Trains lateral hip range, adductor extensibility, and ankle dorsiflexion under load.

*🟢 Recreational:* Hold a doorframe or pole for support; smaller depth.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Hold a kettlebell at the chest (8–16kg).

*Contraindications:* Recent knee meniscus injury — limit depth. Use caution if a history of meniscus issues.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Front-on Cossack at bottom of loaded side.\]

---

**Exercise 5 — Standing Forward Fold**

*Sets/Reps:* 60 seconds.

*How:* Feet hip-width. Soft bend in the knees. Hinge at the hips and let the upper body hang heavy. Hold opposite elbows. Sway gently.

*Key focus:* The knees stay soft. The aim is to release the back, not max out the hamstring.

*Why:* Decompression for the spine and a passive posterior-chain reset.

*🟢 Recreational:* Hands on shins, more knee bend.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Slowly straighten one leg at a time while the other stays soft, 5 alternations.

*Contraindications:* Recent disc symptoms. (See history-of-disc rule on the disclaimer page.)

---

**Exercise 6 — Diaphragmatic Breathing**

*Sets/Reps:* 5 minutes.

*How:* Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. One hand on the chest, one on the belly. Inhale through the nose for 4 seconds — the belly hand rises, the chest hand stays still. Exhale through the mouth for 6 seconds — the belly hand falls.

*Key focus:* Quiet chest, busy belly. Long exhale.

*Why:* Down-regulates the nervous system, locks in the day's mobility work, and trains the diaphragm — an under-trained muscle in most desk-based adults. Note: diaphragmatic breathing on Day 3 is for activation and capacity; box breathing on Day 5 is for down-regulation. Both are slow-paced breathing patterns with different ratio biases — different tools, different jobs.

*🟢🟡🔴:* Same for all levels. Five full minutes is the dose.

### DAY 4 (Weeks 1-2) — Ankles & Deep Squat

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Header image: deep squat hold from the side.\]

**Why this session:** Ankles get ignored, and they are one of the most common limiters of squat depth, lunge mechanics, and running economy.

**Total time:** 20 minutes.

---

**Exercise 1 — Ankle CARs (Controlled Articular Rotations)**

*Sets/Reps:* 8 slow circles in each direction, each ankle. ~90 seconds total.

*How:* Sit or stand with one foot lifted. Draw the largest, slowest circle you can with the toes, working the full available range — point, in, dorsiflex, out. Reverse. Then plant both feet and lift only the toes (not the heels) up and down, 15 reps.

*Key focus:* Big slow circles. The smaller and faster you go, the less it does.

*Why:* CARs wake up the small foot and ankle stabilisers, prime circulation, and train the ankle to own its full range — exactly what carries over to every squat, lunge, and step.

*🟢🟡🔴:* Same for all levels — but make the circles big and slow.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Foot mid-circle and toes-lifted figure side-by-side.\]

---

**Exercise 2 — Wall Ankle Mobilisation**

*Sets/Reps:* 10 reps each side.

*How:* Half-kneeling facing a wall, front foot 10cm from the wall. Drive the front knee over the toes toward the wall, keeping the heel down. Tap the wall and return.

*Key focus:* Heel stays planted. Knee tracks over the *second toe*, not collapsing inward.

*Why:* A direct, well-evidenced way to train ankle dorsiflexion under closed-chain conditions.

*🟢 Recreational:* Foot 7cm from wall.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Foot 10cm from wall.
*🔴 Athlete:* Foot 12cm+ from wall, with a band around the front of the ankle pulling backward (band-assisted dorsiflexion).

*Contraindications:* Recent ankle sprain — check with your physio first. Recent or active Achilles tendinopathy — back off depth and consult a physio.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on half-kneel knee-to-wall with arrow on heel and knee.\]

---

**Exercise 3 — Deep Squat Hold**

*Sets/Reps:* 60 seconds, 3 rounds.

*How:* Drop into the deepest squat you can hold without your heels lifting. Hands together at the chest, elbows can press the inside of the knees.

*Key focus:* Heels down. Long spine. Active feet — grip the floor with all five toes.

*Why:* The deep squat is a position humans should own. This rebuilds tolerance and capacity in it.

*🟢 Recreational:* Hold a doorframe or pole for support.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Hold a kettlebell goblet-style (8–16kg). Add slow rotations to one side, then the other, breathing into the bottom.

*Contraindications:* Recent knee or low back pain — reduce depth. Recent or active Achilles tendinopathy — reduce depth and consult a physio.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Front-on deep squat hold, hands at chest.\]

---

**Exercise 4 — Tempo Squat (Bodyweight or Goblet)**

*Sets/Reps:* 8 reps, 4-second descent, 2-second pause at the bottom, 2-second drive up.

*How:* Bodyweight squat (or hold a light weight at the chest if you've earned 🔴). Lower for a 4-count, pause for 2 at the bottom, drive up for a 2-count.

*Key focus:* Control on the way down. Knees track over the second toe. The pause at the bottom is non-negotiable.

*Why:* Builds active control through the full squat range, exactly what the deep squat hold has just opened up.

*🟢 Recreational:* Bodyweight, partial range.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Bodyweight, full range.
*🔴 Athlete:* Goblet-loaded with 8–16kg kettlebell or dumbbell.

*Contraindications:* Recent knee pain.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on goblet squat at bottom position.\]

---

**Exercise 5 — Standing Calf Stretch**

*Sets/Reps:* 60 seconds each side. 30s straight knee, 30s bent knee.

*How:* Stand a step away from a wall, hands on the wall. Step one foot back, heel planted, leg straight. Hold 30 seconds. Then bend the back knee slightly while keeping the heel down. Hold 30 seconds.

*Key focus:* Straight knee = gastrocnemius. Bent knee = soleus. Both matter.

*Why:* Two muscles, two stretches. Most people only do one and wonder why their ankles are still tight.

*🟢🟡🔴:* Same for all levels.

*Contraindications:* Recent or active Achilles tendinopathy — back off the depth and consult a physio.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-by-side: straight-knee and bent-knee calf stretch.\]

---

**Exercise 6 — Couch Stretch**

*Sets/Reps:* 60 seconds each side.

*How:* Kneel facing away from a wall or couch — **place a folded towel or pad under the kneeling kneecap**. Place the shin against the wall, top of the foot resting on the wall, toes pointing up toward the ceiling. Step the other foot forward into a half-lunge. Squeeze the back glute and tuck the tailbone *before* you sit upright.

*Key focus:* Glute squeeze + tailbone tuck *before* you come tall. Without that, you're loading your lumbar spine, not stretching the quad.

*Why:* A strong stretch for the rectus femoris and TFL — the front-of-thigh muscles that get crushed by sitting and running.

*🟢 Recreational:* Smaller range — don't sit upright; stay leaned forward over the front leg.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Add a contract-relax — push the back foot into the wall for 5s, relax for 10s. 4 cycles per side.

*Contraindications:* Recent knee pain — substitute a half-kneeling hip flexor stretch.

### DAY 5 (Weeks 1-2) — Posterior Chain & Wind-Down

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Header image: figure mid single-leg RDL.\]

**Why this session:** End the week by re-engaging the posterior chain — glutes, hamstrings, low back — and wind down the nervous system.

**Total time:** 20 minutes.

---

**Exercise 1 — Glute Bridge → March**

*Sets/Reps:* 10 bridges, then 10 marches (5 per leg).

*How:* Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Squeeze the glutes and lift the hips until shoulders, hips, and knees are in a line. Lower with control. After 10 reps, hold the bridge up and march one knee in toward the chest at a time — table-top height (90° at the hip) is the target — hips staying level.

*Key focus:* The lift comes from the *glutes* squeezing, not from arching the low back. Hips stay level during the march.

*Why:* Re-activates the glutes, a commonly under-active muscle group in sedentary populations and a frequent feature of chronic low-back pain populations (Cooper et al., 2016, *J Back Musculoskelet Rehabil*).

*🟢 Recreational:* Bridges only, skip the march.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Single-leg bridge — one foot on the floor, the other leg extended.

*Contraindications:* Recent low back pain on extension.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Bridge top position with hip line annotation.\]

---

**Exercise 2 — Bird Dog**

*Sets/Reps:* 8 reps each side, slow.

*How:* On hands and knees. Reach the right arm forward and the left leg back at the same time, slowly. Hold 2 seconds. Return without crashing. Switch sides.

*Key focus:* No twisting. The hips stay square, the spine stays neutral. Imagine a glass of water on your low back — don't spill it.

*Why:* Trains anti-rotation core control with hip and shoulder extension — a McGill-tested staple of lumbar-spine safety work.

*🟢 Recreational:* Lift just the leg (no arm), or just the arm (no leg).
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Add an end-range pulse — at full extension, pulse 3 small reps before returning.

*Contraindications:* Recent low back pain.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on bird dog with neutral-spine line.\]

---

**Exercise 3 — Single-Leg RDL (Bodyweight)**

*Sets/Reps:* 8 reps each side, slow.

*How:* Stand on one leg, soft knee. Hinge at the hip, sending the free leg back behind you and the chest forward, keeping a long line from heel to head. Return by squeezing the standing glute.

*Key focus:* Long spine. Hips stay square — no rotating the back hip open.

*Why:* Trains hamstring length under control while challenging single-leg balance and posterior chain coordination.

*🟢 Recreational:* Hold a wall or doorframe for balance.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Hold a kettlebell or dumbbell in the opposite hand to the standing leg.

*Contraindications:* Recent hamstring strain.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on single-leg RDL at bottom position.\]

---

**Exercise 4 — Standing Hamstring Stretch with Block**

*Sets/Reps:* 60 seconds each side.

*How:* Place one heel on a low block, step, or stair. Foot flexed. Hinge at the hip toward the elevated leg, long spine.

*Key focus:* The hinge is from the hip, not the spine. Don't round to chase your toes — that just stretches your low back.

*Why:* Targets the hamstring without dumping load into the lumbar spine.

*🟢 Recreational:* Lower step, soft knee.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Add a slow nerve glide — point and flex the foot 10 times within the hold.

*Contraindications:* Recent sciatic nerve symptoms — reduce the stretch and don't force the foot flex.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on hinge to elevated foot on block.\]

---

**Exercise 5 — Child's Pose with Side Reach**

*Sets/Reps:* 60 seconds each side.

*How:* Kneel, big toes together, knees wide. Sit back to the heels and reach forward. Walk both hands to one side without lifting the opposite hip.

*Key focus:* Both hips stay anchored. The reach is sideways through the lats and obliques.

*Why:* Opens the lats and quadratus lumborum — the side-of-the-trunk muscles that lock down after a hard week.

*🟢🟡🔴:* Same for all levels.

*Contraindications:* Recent knee flexion pain — substitute a seated lat stretch holding a doorframe. Recent disc symptoms — keep range conservative. (See history-of-disc rule on the disclaimer page.)

**Exercise 6 — Box Breathing Wind-Down**

*Sets/Reps:* 5 minutes — 4-second inhale, 4-second hold, 4-second exhale, 4-second hold. Approximately 18–20 cycles.

*How:* Sit or lie down. Inhale 4s through the nose. Hold 4s. Exhale 4s through the mouth. Hold 4s. Repeat.

*Key focus:* Quiet chest, smooth transitions, no straining.

*Why:* Locks in nervous-system down-regulation. Slow-paced breathing with held phases shifts autonomic balance toward parasympathetic activity (Russo et al., 2017).

*🟢🟡🔴:* Same for all levels.

## Part 5 — Weeks 3–4: Progression

### DAY 1 (Weeks 3–4) — Hips & Hamstrings, Loaded

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Header image: 90/90 hip lift with active arm.\]

**Why this session:** You spent Weeks 1–2 teaching your nervous system that deep hip positions are safe. Now you load them. Cossack squats with a kettlebell, PAILs/RAILs in the half-kneeling hip flexor, active end-range internal rotation in 90/90 — this is where the early structural adaptation (capsule, tendon, connective tissue) gets reinforced.

**Total time:** 25–30 minutes. **Warm-up first:** 2–3 minutes of brisk walking, jumping jacks, or skipping.

---

**Exercise 1 — Hip CARs**

*Sets/Reps:* 5 slow circles in each direction, each leg. ~2 minutes total.

*How:* On hands and knees. Lift one knee out to the side at 90°, then circle it forward, up, and back behind you, returning to the start. Move *slowly* and with deliberate tension — make the joint do the work, not momentum.

*Key focus:* Other three limbs stay still. Pelvis stays level. By Week 3, your circles should be visibly larger than they were on Day 1 of Week 1.

*Why:* CARs prime the hip for the loaded work that follows in this session.

*🟢 Recreational:* Smaller circles, hand on a chair for support if needed.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Full active range, slow and deliberate.
*🔴 Athlete:* Add a 2-second pause at the top of each circle (max external rotation), breathing into the position.

*Contraindications:* Recent hip surgery or labral repair — clear with your physio first.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on figure on all fours, leg arcing through CAR pattern.\]

---

**Exercise 2 — World's Greatest Stretch + Rotation Hold**

*Sets/Reps:* 5 reps each side, with a 5-second hold at end-range rotation.

*How:* From a push-up position, step your right foot outside your right hand. Lower the right elbow toward the inside of the right foot. Hold one breath. Then rotate the right arm up to the ceiling, eyes following the hand. Hold the top of the rotation for 5 seconds, breathing into the side ribs. Return and switch.

*Key focus:* Keep the back leg long and the back knee off the floor. The 5-second hold is non-negotiable — that's what makes this an end-range training rep instead of a warm-up rep.

*Why:* Loads the hip flexor, adductor, hamstring, and Thoracic Spine in one movement. The 5-second top hold trains active control at the end range of thoracic rotation.

*🟢 Recreational:* Drop the back knee to the floor; reduce the hold to 2 seconds.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Back knee off the floor; full 5-second hold.
*🔴 Athlete:* Add a deeper drop — bring the elbow all the way to the floor — and hold the rotation 5 seconds without losing depth.

*Contraindications:* Recent hip flexor strain.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Three-frame sequence: lunge entry, elbow drop, rotation reach with 5s hold.\]

---

**Exercise 3 — 90/90 with Active Knee Lift**

*Sets/Reps:* 8 switches, lifting the front knee 5cm off the floor at the top of each switch.

*How:* Sit on the floor. Front leg bent 90° in front of you, back leg bent 90° to the side. Both shins on the floor. Sit tall. Lift both knees, rotate to the other side, and *before* setting the new front shin down, actively lift the front knee 5cm off the floor and hold for 1 second. Lower with control. Switch sides.

*Key focus:* The knee lift is *active* — drive it up using the deep hip rotators, don't kick it up with momentum. Sit upright throughout. If you collapse forward, the lift becomes a hip flexor compensation.

*Why:* Trains end-range internal hip rotation under active control — a commonly under-developed range in lifters and runners, and a useful contributor to clean squat depth.

*🟢 Recreational:* Hands down for support, smaller switch range, skip the active knee lift — just settle into the new position.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Hands off, sit tall, full 5cm active knee lift.
*🔴 Athlete:* Hands off, full active knee lift held for 2 seconds at the top of each switch (active CAR variation).

*Contraindications:* Recent knee meniscus injury. Use a cushion under the knees if needed.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Top-down 90/90 with arrow showing switch direction and knee lift annotation.\]

---

**Exercise 4 — Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor + PAILs/RAILs**

*Sets/Reps:* 60 seconds passive hold, then 2 cycles of (20s PAILs + 20s RAILs) per side. ~3 minutes per side.

*How:* Half-kneeling position (use a pad under the kneeling knee). Squeeze the back glute hard. Tuck your tailbone under (posterior pelvic tilt). Shift slightly forward and reach the same-side arm overhead, bending slightly to the opposite side. Hold this passive stretch for 60 seconds, breathing into the front of the back hip.

Then cycle:

- **PAILs (20s):** Press the back knee *down* into the floor at moderate effort (50–60%). You're contracting the hip flexor that's being stretched. Keep the tailbone tucked. Breathe.
- **RAILs (20s):** Reverse — actively *lift* the back knee an inch off the floor (or try to), driving from the back glute and hamstring. You're contracting the antagonists to the hip flexor, asking the body to pull deeper into hip extension under its own power.

Repeat the (20s PAILs + 20s RAILs) cycle twice per side. By the second RAILs cycle, your back glute should be screaming. Good.

*Key focus:* Tailbone stays tucked the whole time. If the low back hyperextends during PAILs or RAILs, you're loading the lumbar spine instead of the hip flexor — back off the effort and re-tuck.

*Why:* The 60-second hold is neural-tolerance work. The PAILs/RAILs cycles are connective-tissue and active-control work, drawn from the FRC methodology (Spina). Together they teach your body to *own* the deeper range, not just visit it.

*🟢 Recreational:* 60-second passive hold only — skip the PAILs/RAILs. Build up to 1 cycle by the end of Week 4.
*🟡 Intermediate:* 60s hold + 1 PAILs/RAILs cycle per side.
*🔴 Athlete:* 60s hold + 2 PAILs/RAILs cycles per side, working effort up to 60–70% on the second cycle.

*Contraindications:* Recent anterior hip impingement or hip flexor strain. Use caution if a history of either — reduce range and avoid forcing forward.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on half-kneel showing passive hold, then PAILs (knee pressing down), then RAILs (knee actively lifting).\]

---

**Exercise 5 — Frog with Hip Rocks**

*Sets/Reps:* 60-second hold, then 8 slow forward-and-back hip rocks. 1 round.

*How:* On hands and knees. Widen the knees as far as comfortable, feet in line with the knees. Lower the hips back toward the heels and drop onto the forearms. Hold for 60 seconds, breathing slowly into the inner hips. Then begin slow hip rocks — shift the hips forward toward the hands, then back toward the heels — moving through your full available range. 8 reps, ~3 seconds in each direction.

*Key focus:* Long, slow exhales during the hold. During the rocks, move through the *full* range you have — don't shortcut. The point is to load the new range with movement, not bounce in and out of the bottom.

*Why:* The hold opens the adductors and inner hip capsule; the rocks lock in the new range.

*🟢 Recreational:* Knees narrower, hips higher, hold only — skip the rocks.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written — full hold + 8 slow rocks.
*🔴 Athlete:* Wider knees, deeper hold, then 8 rocks moving through the *new* depth you've earned. Keep the rocks slow — no momentum.

*Contraindications:* Recent inner thigh strain. Do not perform during pregnancy without physio clearance (see disclaimer).

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Front-on figure on forearms in frog position with rock-direction arrow.\]

**Exercise 6 — Cossack Squat — Loaded**

**Exercise 7 — Seated Figure-4 — Loaded**

### DAY 2 (Weeks 3–4) — Thoracic Spine & Shoulders, Loaded

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Header image: kettlebell halo at shoulder height.\]

**Why this session:** Foundation taught your Thoracic Spine to rotate and extend. Now you load the rotation, weight the extension, and add new patterns (the Halo) that integrate shoulder and Thoracic Spine under load.

**Total time:** 25–30 minutes. **Warm-up first:** 2–3 minutes of brisk walking, jumping jacks, or skipping.

**Exercise 1 — Shoulder CARs**

*Sets/Reps:* 5 slow circles in each direction, each arm. ~2 minutes total.

*How:* Standing tall. Reach one arm forward, then up overhead, then back behind you, then down — drawing the biggest, slowest circle the joint allows. Keep the rest of the body locked in place. Reverse direction.

*Key focus:* Rib cage stays still. Pelvis stays still. By Week 3, your circles should reach further behind your body than they did in Week 1.

*Why:* CARs train the shoulder to own its full active range — exactly what protects you when you reach overhead under load (which you're about to do with the Halo).

*🟢 Recreational:* Smaller circles, focus on quality over range.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Full active range, slow and deliberate.
*🔴 Athlete:* Add a 2-second pause at the top of each circle (max external rotation overhead), breathing into the position.

**Exercise 2 — Thread the Needle + Open-Arm Version**

*Sets/Reps:* 8 reps each side.

*How:* On hands and knees. Reach the right arm under the body, threading it past the left arm. Lower the right shoulder and ear toward the floor. Return to start by reaching the right arm up to the ceiling, opening the chest into rotation. The "open-arm" 🔴 progression: place the supporting hand behind the head and drive the elbow up to the ceiling, then under and across the body.

*Key focus:* The reach up is as important as the reach under. If the hand-behind-head version makes you compensate by twisting through the lumbar, drop back to the closed-arm version.

*Why:* Closed-chain thoracic rotation — trains Thoracic Spine rotation away from the lumbar spine.

*🟢 Recreational:* Closed-arm version, supporting elbow soft.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Closed-arm with a 3-second hold at end of rotation in each direction.
*🔴 Athlete:* Open-arm version — supporting hand behind the head, elbow drives up and under.

**Exercise 3 — Thoracic Extension Over Roller, Loaded**

*Sets/Reps:* 60 seconds, 3 rounds. Hold a 2.5–5kg plate to the chest.

*How:* Foam roller (or rolled towel) horizontally under the upper back at shoulder-blade level. Hold a weight plate on your chest with both hands. Slowly arch back over the roller, breathe out, return. Move the roller up one segment after each round.

*Key focus:* Extend through the *upper* back, not the low back. The hips stay grounded. The plate adds load to the extension — let gravity drive the stretch, don't push.

*Why:* Loaded thoracic extension restores range that desk and phone posture have stolen. The plate is what makes this Week 3–4 work — the unloaded version is a Week 1 movement.

*🟢 Recreational:* Unloaded — support the head fully, smaller range.
*🟡 Intermediate:* 2.5kg plate held to the chest.

**Exercise 4 — Quadruped Thoracic Spine Rotation + 3s Hold**

*Sets/Reps:* 8 reps each side, with a 3-second pause at the top of each rotation.

*How:* On hands and knees. Place one hand behind the head. Drive that elbow up toward the ceiling, rotating through the mid-back. Hold the top for 3 seconds, breathing into the side ribs. Then drive the elbow under and across the body.

*Key focus:* Hips stay square. The 3-second hold at the top is where the training adaptation lives.

*Why:* Isolates Thoracic Spine rotation away from the lumbar spine. The hold trains active control at end range.

*🟢 Recreational:* Smaller range, no hold — just slow up-and-under reps.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Full range, 3-second hold at the top.
*🔴 Athlete:* Full range, 3-second hold + an active breath into the side ribs at the top of each rotation.

**Exercise 5 — Banded Pass-Through, Tighter Grip**

*Sets/Reps:* 10 reps, 2 rounds. Move the hands closer together than you did in Week 1.

*How:* Hold a band wide overhead. Pass the band over the head and behind the back without bending the elbows. Reverse. The progression in Week 3–4 is to *narrow your grip* — the closer your hands, the more shoulder mobility is required.

*Key focus:* Don't compensate by arching the low back off neutral, or by shrugging the shoulders. If you can't keep elbows locked, your grip is too narrow — widen until you can move cleanly.

*Why:* Trains shoulder flexion and external rotation through the deepest range your nervous system trusts. Narrowing the grip systematically expands that trust.

*🟢 Recreational:* Wall slide instead — narrower or partial range.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Wide-grip banded pass-through, full range.
*🔴 Athlete:* Narrow-grip banded pass-through, full range, no elbow bend.

*Contraindications:* Recent shoulder impingement or anterior instability. Use caution if a history of either.

**Exercise 6 — Doorway Pec Stretch + PAILs/RAILs**

*Sets/Reps:* 60 seconds passive hold, then 2 cycles of (20s PAILs + 20s RAILs) per side.

*How:* Stand in a doorway. Place one forearm against the frame, elbow at shoulder height (90°). Step the same-side foot through the door and rotate the chest away. Hold the passive stretch for 60 seconds, breathing into the chest.

Then cycle:

- **PAILs (20s):** Press the forearm *into* the doorframe at moderate effort (50–60%). You're contracting the pec that's being stretched. Don't let the body shift back toward the door.
- **RAILs (20s):** Stop pressing into the doorframe. Now actively rotate the chest *away* from the door, retracting and depressing the shoulder blade — squeeze the rhomboids and lower trap to pull yourself deeper into the stretch under your own power.

Repeat the cycle twice per side.

*Key focus:* Shoulder blade stays back and down throughout. Stretching a slack pec changes very little; stretching a pec while actively retracting the scapula changes a lot.

*Why:* Loaded chest opening. The PAILs phase loads the pec at end range; the RAILs phase trains the antagonist (mid-back) to pull deeper.

*🟢 Recreational:* 60-second passive hold only — skip PAILs/RAILs. Lower elbow position for less stretch.
*🟡 Intermediate:* 60s hold + 1 PAILs/RAILs cycle per side.
*🔴 Athlete:* 60s hold + 2 PAILs/RAILs cycles per side, with effort building toward 60–70% on the second cycle.

*Contraindications:* Recent anterior shoulder instability. Skip the PAILs/RAILs cycles entirely if any history of shoulder impingement or dislocation — passive hold only.

**Exercise 7 — Kettlebell Halo**

*Sets/Reps:* 5 slow circles in each direction.

*How:* Hold a kettlebell upside-down by the horns at chest height (or hold a dumbbell vertically). Slowly circle the bell around the head — pass it close behind the neck, then around to the front, keeping the elbows wide and the shoulders down. Reverse direction after 5 reps.

*Key focus:* Slow. The whole point is shoulder control through the awkward overhead-and-behind-the-head positions. If the bell lurches or the elbows collapse, you're moving too fast or going too heavy.

*Why:* Integrates shoulder mobility with rotator cuff strength under load. Trains the back-of-the-head position that overhead lifting demands.

*🟢 Recreational:* Unloaded "halo" — clasp your hands together and circle them around the head, learning the path.
*🟡 Intermediate:* 4–8kg kettlebell or dumbbell.
*🔴 Athlete:* 8–12kg kettlebell, slow and controlled, 5 reps each direction.

*Contraindications:* Recent neck or shoulder injury. If you can't keep the bell off the back of your head, drop the load.

### DAY 3 (Weeks 3–4) — Full-Body Flow, Athletic

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Header image: Cossack with overhead reach.\]

**Why this session:** Day 3 connects everything you've trained in the other four sessions and lets you express it as a continuous flow. This is the integration day — moving cleanly between positions matters more than spending long in any one of them.

**Total time:** 25–30 minutes. **Warm-up first:** 2–3 minutes of brisk walking, jumping jacks, or skipping.

---

**Exercise 1 — Sun Salutation A Flow (Full Sequence)**

*Sets/Reps:* 5 full flows.

*How:* The full Sun A sequence is:

1. **Mountain pose** — stand tall, arms by sides, breathe.
2. **Forward fold** — exhale, hinge at the hips, hands toward the floor.
3. **Halfway lift** — inhale, lift the chest halfway, long spine, fingertips on shins.
4. **High plank** — exhale, step or jump back to a strong plank.
5. **Low plank (chaturanga)** — lower halfway, elbows hugging the ribs.
6. **Upward dog** — inhale, roll over the toes, lift the chest, hips just off the floor.
7. **Down dog** — exhale, push back into an inverted V, heels reaching toward the floor. Hold for one full breath.
8. **Step or jump forward** to a forward fold.
9. **Halfway lift** — inhale, long spine.
10. **Mountain** — exhale, return to standing.

That's one flow. Do 5.

*Key focus:* Match the breath to the movement. Each transition should be one breath. The down dog hold (one full breath) is the rest stop in each rep — use it.

*Why:* A continuous flow that links shoulder control, plank stability, hip extension, posterior chain, and breath in one pattern.

*🟢 Recreational:* Knees-down chaturanga; skip up-dog, substitute cobra (knees and hips down).
*🟡 Intermediate:* Full sequence as written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Full sequence with a 3-second hold at the peak of each pose (down dog, up dog, halfway lift).

*Contraindications:* Recent wrist injury — substitute cobra to baby cobra (sphinx) pose to standing fold (skip plank/chaturanga). Recent low back pain or history of disc symptoms — keep the up-dog conservative or substitute cobra throughout.

**Exercise 2 — Inchworm with Push-Up + Squat Reach**

*Sets/Reps:* 8 reps. One integrated rep = walkout + push-up + walk-in + squat reach.

*Key focus:* Soft knees on the walkout (don't lock them). The push-up is a *clean* push-up — full range, elbows tracking toward the ribs. In the squat, lift the chest and rotate from the mid-back, not the low back.

*Why:* Connects posterior chain (walkout/walk-in), shoulder and chest stability (push-up), deep squat (bottom hold), and Thoracic Spine rotation (overhead reach) in a single integrated rep.

*🟢 Recreational:* Skip the push-up and the squat reach — just walk out, walk in, stand up.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Add the push-up. Skip the squat reach.
*🔴 Athlete:* Full sequence — walkout + push-up + walk-in + deep squat with overhead reach.

*Contraindications:* Recent low back pain on flexion or history of disc symptoms — keep walkout shallow, skip the deep squat reach. (See history-of-disc rule on the disclaimer page.) Recent wrist or shoulder injury — skip the push-up.

**Exercise 4 — Cossack Squat with Overhead Reach**

**Exercise 5 — Standing Forward Fold, Alternating Legs**

Then begin to slowly straighten one leg while the other knee stays bent — feel the hamstring on the straightening side. Hold 3 seconds. Soften that knee, straighten the other. ### DAY 4 (Weeks 3–4) — Ankles & Deep Squat, Loaded

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Header image: loaded goblet squat at depth.\]

**Why this session:** Foundation built ankle range. Now you load the squat that depends on it. The big movements — loaded deep squat hold, tempo goblet squat, ATG split squat — need loaded ankle prep first (CARs, banded mob).

**Total time:** 25–30 minutes. **Warm-up first:** 2–3 minutes of brisk walking, jumping jacks, or skipping.

---

**Exercise 1 — Ankle CARs**

*Sets/Reps:* 8 slow circles in each direction, each foot. ~2 minutes total.

*How:* Sit or stand with one foot lifted. Draw the largest, slowest circle you can with the toes, working the full available range — point, in, dorsiflex, out. Reverse direction.

*Key focus:* Big slow circles. By Week 3 your ankles should trace a visibly bigger circle than they did in Week 1.

*Why:* Wakes up the small foot and ankle stabilisers, primes circulation, and trains the ankle to own its full range — exactly what carries over to every loaded squat and lunge that follows in this session.

*🟢 Recreational:* Smaller slow circles, focus on quality of range.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Full active range, slow.
*🔴 Athlete:* Add a 2-second pause at end-range dorsiflexion (toes pulled hardest toward shin) on each circle.

*Contraindications:* Recent ankle sprain or surgery — clear with your physio first.

**Exercise 2 — Banded Wall Ankle Mobilisation**

*Sets/Reps:* 10 reps each side.

*How:* Half-kneeling facing a wall, front foot positioned per your tier (see below). Wrap a resistance band around the front of the ankle, anchored to a low point behind you, so the band pulls the front of the ankle backward (creating posterior glide of the talus). Drive the front knee over the toes toward the wall, keeping the heel down. Tap the wall and return.

*Key focus:* Heel stays planted. Knee tracks over the *second toe*. The band's job is to pull the talus back during dorsiflexion — a joint-mobilisation effect that the unbanded version doesn't get.

*Why:* The band-assisted version targets the joint glide that limits dorsiflexion in chronically stiff ankles.

*🟢 Recreational:* No band, foot 7cm from wall.
*🟡 Intermediate:* No band, foot 10cm from wall.
*🔴 Athlete:* Band around the front of the ankle, foot 12cm+ from wall.

*Contraindications:* Recent ankle sprain — check with your physio first. Recent or active Achilles tendinopathy — back off depth and consult a physio.

**Exercise 3 — Deep Squat Hold — Loaded**

*Sets/Reps:* 60 seconds, 3 rounds. Hold a kettlebell goblet-style. Within each round, add slow rotations to one side, then the other (🔴 only).

*How:* Hold a kettlebell at the chest. Drop into the deepest squat you can hold without your heels lifting. Hands stay on the bell at the chest, elbows can press the inside of the knees. Hold 60 seconds. On the 🔴 tier, add slow controlled rotations — turn the chest left for 3 seconds, return, turn right for 3 seconds — within the hold.

*Key focus:* Heels down. Long spine. Active feet — grip the floor with all five toes. The load deepens the position; let it. Don't fight the bell up.

*Why:* Loading the deep squat builds tolerance, capsular adaptation, and bottom-position strength that transfers directly to every squat variation.

*🟢 Recreational:* Unloaded, doorframe or pole for support.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Unloaded, free hands at chest.
*🔴 Athlete:* 8–16kg goblet, 60s hold per round, with slow 3-second rotations to each side within each round.

*Contraindications:* Recent knee or low back pain — reduce depth. Recent or active Achilles tendinopathy — reduce depth and consult a physio.

**Exercise 4 — Tempo Squat (Bodyweight or Goblet)**

*Sets/Reps:* 8 reps, 4-second descent, 2-second pause at the bottom, 2-second drive up.

*How:* Stand with feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out. Hold a kettlebell goblet-style at the chest (🔴 tier) or just stand tall (🟢/🟡). Lower for a 4-count, pause for 2 seconds at the bottom, drive up smoothly for 2 counts.

*Key focus:* Control on the way down — don't drop. Knees track over the second toe, heels stay grounded. The 2-second pause at the bottom is non-negotiable.

*Why:* Builds active strength through the full squat range, exactly the range the deep squat hold has just opened. Loaded tempo work is what turns mobility into usable strength.

*🟢 Recreational:* Bodyweight, partial range — squat only as deep as you can hold form.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Bodyweight, full range to the bottom of your deep squat.
*🔴 Athlete:* Goblet-loaded with 8–16kg kettlebell or dumbbell.

*Contraindications:* Recent knee pain — reduce depth and load. Recent low back pain — focus on the 4-second descent and skip the bottom pause if it loads the spine.

**Exercise 5 — ATG Split Squat (or Reverse Lunge)**

*Sets/Reps:* 6 reps each side, slow.

*How (ATG split squat):* Stand in a split stance, front foot flat, back foot on the toes. Lower the back knee toward (or to) the floor while keeping the front heel planted and the front knee tracking over the toes. The "ATG" — Ass-To-Grass — version drives the front knee well past the toes. This is safe in this controlled, full-range pattern: the "knees-over-toes" caution is a coaching cue for sloppy squats, not a biomechanical law (Fry, Smith & Schilling, 2003, *J Strength Cond Res*, showed restricting forward knee travel substantially increased hip torque while only modestly reducing knee torque). Stand back up by driving through the front foot.

*How (reverse lunge — 🟢 alternative):* From standing, step one foot back into a lunge, drop the back knee toward the floor, drive back up to standing. Switch sides each rep.

*Key focus:* Front heel stays planted throughout. Front knee tracks over the second toe. In the ATG version, the front shin angles forward — that's the point.

*Why:* Builds knee-over-toe strength and tolerance, which contributes to athletic single-leg power, deep squatting, and durable knees.

*🟢 Recreational:* Reverse lunge, bodyweight — back knee to the floor, no front-knee-over-toe.
*🟡 Intermediate:* ATG split squat, bodyweight, full range.
*🔴 Athlete:* ATG split squat with **4–8kg in each hand** (i.e. 8–16kg total). Alternatively, hold a single 8–12kg KB goblet-style at the chest. Front foot can be elevated 10cm on a plate or step for more range.

*Contraindications:* Recent knee or patellar tendon pain — drop to the reverse lunge variation. Avoid front-foot elevation if you have a history of patellar tendinopathy.

**Exercise 6 — Standing Calf Stretch (Straight + Bent Knee)**

*Sets/Reps:* 60 seconds each side. 30 seconds straight knee, 30 seconds bent knee.

*How:* Stand a step away from a wall, hands on the wall. Step one foot back, heel planted, leg straight. Hold 30 seconds. Then bend the back knee slightly while keeping the heel down. Hold 30 seconds. Switch sides.

*Key focus:* Straight knee = gastrocnemius (the upper calf muscle, which crosses the knee). Bent knee = soleus (the deeper, knee-non-crossing calf). Both matter, both load the Achilles.

*Why:* Two muscles, two stretches. After a session of loaded squatting, both heads of the calf appreciate the dose.

*Levels:* Same for everyone.

*Contraindications:* Recent or active Achilles tendinopathy — back off the depth and consult a physio.

**Exercise 7 — Couch Stretch + PAILs/RAILs**

*Sets/Reps:* 60 seconds passive hold, then 2 cycles of (20s PAILs + 20s RAILs) per side.

*How:* Kneel facing away from a wall or couch. Place a folded towel or pad under the kneeling kneecap. Place the shin against the wall, top of the foot resting on the wall, toes pointing up toward the ceiling. Step the other foot forward into a half-lunge. Squeeze the back glute and tuck the tailbone *before* you sit upright. Hold the passive stretch for 60 seconds.

Then cycle:

- **PAILs (20s):** Press the top of the back foot *into* the wall at moderate effort (50–60%). You're contracting the rectus femoris (front of thigh) that's being stretched. Keep the tailbone tucked and the back glute squeezing.
- **RAILs (20s):** Stop pressing into the wall. Now actively squeeze the back glute *harder* and try to drive the hips forward — using the glute (the antagonist to the quad/hip flexor) to pull yourself deeper into hip extension.

Repeat the cycle twice per side. By the second RAILs, the back glute should be on fire.

*Key focus:* Glute squeeze + tailbone tuck *before* you come tall. Without that, you're loading your lumbar spine, not stretching the quad. During PAILs, the press into the wall must not let the tailbone untuck.

*Why:* A direct loaded stretch for the rectus femoris and TFL. PAILs/RAILs trains active hip extension under stretch, which is what protects the lumbar spine in athletic positions.

*🟢 Recreational:* 60-second passive hold only — skip PAILs/RAILs. Smaller range — stay leaned forward over the front leg rather than upright.
*🟡 Intermediate:* 60s hold + 1 PAILs/RAILs cycle per side.
*🔴 Athlete:* 60s hold + 2 PAILs/RAILs cycles per side, with effort building toward 60–70% on the second cycle.

*Contraindications:* Recent knee pain — pad heavily under the kneecap, reduce range, or substitute the half-kneeling hip flexor stretch from Day 1.

### DAY 5 (Weeks 3–4) — Posterior Chain, Loaded

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Header image: loaded single-leg RDL.\]

**Why this session:** End the week with the loaded posterior chain — glutes, hamstrings, low back tolerance, and the controlled spinal flexion that most people avoid (Jefferson curl). Box breathing closes the work week.

**Total time:** 25–30 minutes. **Warm-up first:** 2–3 minutes of brisk walking, jumping jacks, or skipping.

---

**Exercise 1 — Hip Hinge with Dowel**

*Sets/Reps:* 10 reps.

*How:* Hold a dowel, broomstick, or PVC pipe vertically along your spine. Three points of contact: the back of your head, the middle of your upper back (between the shoulder blades), and your tailbone. The dowel must stay in contact with all three points throughout the movement. Standing tall, push the hips back and hinge forward at the hips, knees only slightly bent. Lower until you feel the hamstrings load. Drive the hips forward to return to standing.

*Key focus:* All three contact points stay on the dowel. If your head loses contact, you've extended your neck. If your tailbone loses contact, you've rounded your low back. Both are wrong. The hinge happens at the hips, not the spine.

*Why:* Teaches the neutral-spine hip hinge that every deadlift, RDL, and posterior chain movement is built on. The dowel turns an internal cue ("keep your back straight") into an external one — instant feedback when you cheat.

*🟢 Recreational:* Same for everyone — load is your own bodyweight and the feedback from the dowel.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Same.
*🔴 Athlete:* Same, with deeper hinge and a 2-second pause at the bottom.

*Contraindications:* Recent low back pain — reduce range of hinge, focus on the dowel feedback over depth.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on figure mid-hinge with dowel along the spine, three contact points annotated.\]

---

**Exercise 2 — Single-Leg Glute Bridge**

*Sets/Reps:* 10 reps each side.

*How:* Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Extend one leg straight up toward the ceiling (or out flat in front, your choice). Squeeze the planted-foot glute and lift the hips until shoulders, hips, and knee are in a line. Hold for 1 second at the top. Lower with control.

*Key focus:* The lift comes from the *glute* of the planted leg, not from arching the low back or hiking the hip. Keep the hips level — don't let the unsupported side drop.

*Why:* Loads the glute medius and max in single-leg, which is what stabilises the pelvis in every step you take. Closes asymmetries that the two-leg bridge hides.

*🟢 Recreational:* Two-leg bridge with march (as Week 1 Day 5).
*🟡 Intermediate:* Single-leg bridge as written.
*🔴 Athlete:* Single-leg bridge with a 2-second hold at the top of each rep.

*Contraindications:* Recent low back pain on extension — reduce hip lift.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on single-leg bridge at top position with hip-line annotation.\]

---

**Exercise 3 — Bird Dog with End-Range Pulse**

*Sets/Reps:* 8 reps each side, slow. 3 small pulses at full extension on each rep.

*How:* On hands and knees. Reach the right arm forward and the left leg back at the same time, slowly. At full extension — when the arm and leg are stretched out long — perform 3 small pulses, reaching 1–2cm further on each pulse without losing the neutral spine. Return without crashing. Switch sides.

*Key focus:* No twisting. The hips stay square, the spine stays neutral, the head stays in line with the spine. The pulses are *small* — they're an end-range training stimulus, not a swing.

*Why:* Trains anti-rotation core control with hip and shoulder extension under end-range load. The pulses recruit deep stabilisers that the standard bird dog asks but doesn't fully load.

*🟢 Recreational:* Lift just the leg, or just the arm — no pulses.
*🟡 Intermediate:* Standard arm + leg, 2-second hold, no pulses.
*🔴 Athlete:* Arm + leg with a 3-second hold, then 3 small end-range pulses.

*Contraindications:* Recent low back pain.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on bird dog at full extension with pulse-direction arrows.\]

---

**Exercise 4 — Single-Leg RDL — Loaded**

*Sets/Reps:* 8 reps each side, slow.

*How:* Stand on one leg, soft knee. Hold a dumbbell or kettlebell in the *opposite* hand to the standing leg. Hinge at the hip, sending the free leg back behind you and the chest forward, keeping a long line from heel to head. The weight glides down the standing thigh as you hinge. Return by squeezing the standing glute and driving the hips forward.

*Key focus:* Long spine from head to heel. Hips stay square — don't let the back hip rotate open. The opposite-hand load creates an anti-rotation challenge that the same-hand version doesn't.

*Why:* Trains hamstring length under load while challenging single-leg balance, posterior chain coordination, and rotational control. One of the highest-value movements in the entire program.

*🟢 Recreational:* Bodyweight, hand on a wall or doorframe for balance.
*🟡 Intermediate:* 4–8kg dumbbell in opposite hand.
*🔴 Athlete:* 8–12kg dumbbell in opposite hand, slow tempo (3-second descent).

*Contraindications:* Recent hamstring strain — drop the load and reduce depth.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on single-leg RDL at bottom with weight in opposite hand, long-line annotation.\]

---

**Exercise 5 — Jefferson Curl (Light)**

*Sets/Reps:* 6 reps, 4-second descent, controlled return.

*How:* Stand on a low box or step (or just on the floor) with feet hip-width. Hold a light weight (plate or dumbbell) in both hands in front of you. Slowly roll down — chin to chest first, then segment by segment through the spine, vertebra by vertebra — letting the weight hang toward the floor. Pause briefly at the bottom. Reverse the movement, stacking the spine segment by segment from the bottom up, until standing tall.

*Key focus:* Slow. Segment by segment. The weight is light — this is a tissue-tolerance drill, not a strength lift. If at any point you feel sharp or radiating pain, stop and skip this exercise.

*Why:* Done correctly with light load, builds spinal flexion tolerance through a range most people avoid entirely. Done badly or with the wrong spine, it's a fast track to a flare-up. **Read the Jefferson Curl note below before deciding whether to attempt this exercise.**

*🟢 Recreational:* **SKIP this exercise.** Substitute the Standing Forward Fold (🟢 version, hands on shins) — same general posterior chain bias, no segmental flexion under load.
*🟡 Intermediate:* 2.5kg total load, 6 reps, 4-second descent.
*🔴 Athlete:* 5kg total load, 6 reps, 4-second descent. Stand on a low box (10cm) for slightly more range.

*Contraindications:* History of disc symptoms, sciatica, or any acute back pain — **skip this exercise**, no exceptions. Pregnancy and early postpartum — contraindicated. See the Jefferson Curl Note below.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on three-frame: tall stance, mid-curl (segmental rounding), bottom hang.\]

---

**Exercise 6 — Standing Hamstring Stretch with Nerve Glide**

*Sets/Reps:* 60 seconds each side. Within the hold, point and flex the foot 10 times.

*How:* Place one heel on a low block, step, or stair. Hinge at the hip toward the elevated leg, long spine. Once you feel the hamstring stretch, begin to slowly point the foot (toes away from you) for 2 seconds, then flex (toes toward you) for 2 seconds. Repeat 10 times within the 60-second hold.

*Key focus:* The hinge is from the hip, not the spine. Don't round to chase your toes. The point-flex is a *nerve glide* — slow, no jerk, breathe through it. If you feel sharp pain, tingling, or numbness shoot down the leg, stop the nerve glide and just hold the static stretch.

*Why:* The static hold targets the hamstring; the nerve glide trains the sciatic nerve to slide cleanly through the hamstring tissue, addressing the neural component of hamstring tightness that pure stretching ignores.

*🟢 Recreational:* Lower step, soft knee, hold only — skip the nerve glide.
*🟡 Intermediate:* As written — 60s hold + 10 point-flex.
*🔴 Athlete:* Higher step, deeper hinge, 10 point-flex with each rep moving slightly further into end range.

*Contraindications:* Recent sciatic nerve symptoms — skip the nerve glide entirely, hold only. Stop immediately if symptoms shoot down the leg.

\[ILLUSTRATION PLACEHOLDER — Side-on hinge to elevated foot on block, foot in mid point-flex.\]

---

**Exercise 7 — Box Breathing**

*Sets/Reps:* 5 minutes — 4-second inhale, 4-second hold, 4-second exhale, 4-second hold. Approximately 18–20 cycles.

*How:* Sit or lie down. Inhale 4s through the nose. Hold 4s. Exhale 4s through the mouth. Hold 4s. Repeat.

*Key focus:* Quiet chest, smooth transitions, no straining.

*Why:* Locks in nervous-system down-regulation. Slow-paced breathing with held phases shifts autonomic balance toward parasympathetic activity (Russo et al., 2017).

*Levels:* Same for everyone.

## Jefferson Curl — A Specific Note

The Jefferson curl is a controlled, light-loaded spinal flexion. Done well, it builds tissue tolerance through a range most people avoid entirely. Done badly, or with a spine that doesn't tolerate flexion, it's a fast track to a flare-up.

**Use it if:** you have no current back symptoms, no history of disc-related episodes, and you can already perform a slow segmental bodyweight forward-fold cleanly. Age isn't the variable — symptom history and movement competence are.

**Skip it if:** you have a history of disc symptoms, sciatica, or any acute back pain. Skip during pregnancy and early postpartum. Substitute the Standing Forward Fold (🟢 version, hands on shins).

When in doubt, skip it. There's plenty of mobility work that doesn't carry this caveat. The programme does not need this exercise — it's offered as an option for those who want to actively train spinal flexion tolerance.

## Part 6 — Day 28: Re-Test

The morning after Week 4's last session, run all seven self-assessments again. Same setup, same camera angle, same time of day if you can.

Record the differences in the **Progress Worksheet**. The point of the baseline was to make this moment meaningful — don't skip the re-test.

**What you might see** (these are ranges some people experience — individual variance is large, and depend on starting point, sleep, training load, and consistency. Direction of travel matters more than hitting a number):

- **Deep squat:** heels staying down, longer hold time before form breaks, less effort to get there.
- **Ankle wall test:** some people gain 2–4cm on each side over 28 days; others much less. Side-to-side asymmetry should narrow.
- **Sit and reach:** an additional 3–8cm of reach is on the higher end of what's reported in 4-week protocols.
- **Wall shoulder flexion:** wrists closer to the wall (or touching), low back staying down.
- **Thomas test:** hanging thigh dropping closer to horizontal, knee angle softer (i.e. closer to 80°+).
- **Thoracic Spine rotation:** noticeably more rotation, especially on your stiffer side.
- **Cossack:** deeper depth, heel staying down, foot of the straight leg fully flexed.

If a test went *backwards*, look at sleep, stress, training load outside this programme, and hydration. Mobility responds to recovery — under-recovered ranges feel tighter, not looser.

**Note on timelines:** 28 days produces neural adaptation and the beginnings of structural change. Connective tissue keeps remodelling for weeks beyond — re-test at Day 56 (week 8) and Day 84 (week 12) if you want to see the slower-cooker gains. Many people see their biggest qualitative changes between weeks 8 and 12.

## Part 7 — The Maintenance Plan

You don't need to repeat the 28-day programme to keep your gains. You need a smaller, repeatable dose that fits into the rest of your training.

**Mobility is not a quick fix.** It's a permanent input. The dose drops, the consistency doesn't.

### The Weekly Maintenance Loop (3 mobility touches per week)

**Day 1 — "Daily Five" Hip & Loaded Lower (10 min)**

1. Hip CARs (5 each direction, each leg)
2. Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor + PAILs/RAILs (1 cycle per side)
3. Frog with Hip Rocks (60s + 8 rocks)
4. Cossack Squat with KB (5/side, 8–16kg)
5. 90/90 with Active Knee Lift (8 switches)

**Day 2 — "Daily Five" Ankle, Squat & Posterior (10 min)**

1. Ankle CARs (8 each direction, each foot)
2. Banded Wall Ankle Mobilisation (10/side)
3. Loaded Deep Squat Hold (60s, 8–16kg goblet)
4. Tempo Squat — Goblet (8 reps, 4-down/2-pause/2-up)
5. Couch Stretch + PAILs/RAILs (1 cycle per side)

**Day 3 — Pre-training warm-up (10 min)** Pull from Day 3's flow before any training session.

1. Hip + Shoulder + Ankle CARs (2 min total — non-negotiable opener)
2. Sun Salutation A flow (3 rounds)
3. Inchworm with squat reach (5 reps)
4. World's Greatest Stretch (3/side)
5. Cossack with overhead reach (3/side, light load or unweighted)

**Daily Mobility — One CAR sequence (hip, shoulder, ankle), 2 minutes total.** Do it in the kitchen waiting for the kettle.

### Re-test once per quarter

Every 12 weeks, run the seven assessments again. If a test slips, that's the area to load up next.

### Triggers for repeating the full 28 days

- A test slips meaningfully (for example: you gained 4cm of dorsiflexion and you've lost more than 1cm of it).
- An injury or extended layoff.
- A big new training block (Hyrox prep, marathon training, return to lifting).

You did the work once. Maintenance is the bit most people get wrong — they do the programme, get the gains, then drop everything and lose them by month three. Don't be that person.

## FAQ — The questions everyone asks

**What if I miss a session?** One missed session in 28 days is noise — pick up where you left off, don't stack two sessions to compensate. Two or more missed sessions in a week, restart the week. The point is consistent stimulus, not heroics.

**Should I train through DOMS?** Mobility, yes — it's often the best thing for sore muscles. Skip the loaded end-range work if soreness is sharp or limits clean form. Drop a tier and keep moving.

**What if I'm sick?** Above the neck (head cold, mild sore throat) — keep moving on 🟢, skip the breath holds. Below the neck (chest, fever, body aches) — rest fully until symptoms resolve, then resume from where you stopped.

**Can I split a session into two short blocks?** Yes. 10 minutes morning + 10 minutes evening works — keep the order (CARs first, breath last).

**Can I do this alongside lifting / running / Hyrox prep?** That's exactly the point. See the "When to slot mobility around your other training" guidance in Part 3. Don't stack a long deep-squat hold immediately before max-effort squatting.

**What if I'm travelling and have no equipment?** Run the 🟢 tier of every session. The bodyweight version of this program is a complete program.

**What if I'm pregnant or postpartum?** See the disclaimer. Don't run this version of the program — wait for the companion plan or work with a women's health physio on a custom adaptation.

**Can I keep the maintenance dose forever?** Yes. That's the design. Re-run the full 28 days when a quarterly retest slips, after an injury or extended layoff, or before a big training block.
